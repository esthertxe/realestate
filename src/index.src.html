Got questions? we've got you

Common questions

What is decoupling, and who is it for?

Decoupling generally means one co-owner of a jointly owned property transferring their share to the other. Couples sometimes consider it when planning a further purchase. It carries loan, cash and duty implications that depend on your situation, so the legal steps should be confirmed with a conveyancing lawyer and your bank.

How much can I afford to borrow?

It depends on your income, existing commitments, age, the property type and the lender's own assessment. I can give you an indicative figure to plan with; your bank confirms the actual amount when it assesses your loan application.

How do you compare rental yield?

I look at gross yield, which is annual rent against the price, and net yield after costs such as maintenance, tax, fees, vacancy and financing. Comparing net figures across options gives a more honest picture of what each property returns on what you put in.

What should I prepare for a first consultation?

A rough idea of your income, existing loans, CPF and cash savings, details of any property you own, and what you would like to achieve. Approximate figures are fine; we can refine them together.

What does a property advisor in Singapore do?

A property advisor helps you plan a purchase, sale or change of ownership by working through the numbers with you: costs, financing, cash flow and likely outcomes. I am registered with the Council for Estate Agencies (CEA) and focus on Singapore residential property.

How can I check your CEA registration?
